# Strategy - chemistry/biology, and why the pack split governs everything

Intelligence-rich, resource-poor. We pick problems where a SOTA model plus a workstation plus disciplined verification produces results no one else has. Here half the problems cannot be closed without a wet lab we do not have. The program is organized around one question: where does the verifier live?

## The split, and why it is the whole strategy

- **Pack A - closed-loop.** Ground truth is generated by the machine: a proof, a CAS identity, a physics simulation (QM/MD/GCMC), or synthetic data with a known answer. No new reality needed. Finishable by compute alone. Pack A is our work plan.
- **Pack B - reality-gated.** Ground truth is empirical measurement, fresh (wet-lab assay) or pre-collected (a frozen experimental corpus that is itself the bottleneck). Compute can produce a certified method, a held-out SOTA, and falsifiable predictions, but reality closes the loop. Pack B is a portfolio we resource only alongside a wet-lab partner.

The cut is on verifier location, not discipline: the seam crosses the chemistry/biology line both ways (corpus-gated genomics is Pack B; simulation-verified materials design is Pack A).

## Ranking criteria (within each pack)

1. **Verifier quality** - Pack A: how trustworthy is the on-machine check (an exact proof beats a force field that may be biased); Pack B: does a public, leakage-safe frozen corpus already exist.
2. **On-machine finishability** (Pack A) - formal/algebraic and simulation-verified targets rank above accuracy-ceiling problems that only ever improve.
3. **Start-this-month readiness** - a public verifier or dataset available today beats raw problem importance.
4. **Payoff** - practical utility (functionals that upgrade all downstream chemistry; a route-checker; a clinical variant caller) or foundational leverage.
5. **Bias risk** - penalize problems whose only verifier is a benchmark known to be systematically biased.

## Operating rules

1. **Certified partial results are the product.** Pack A: a verified reduction, a certified constant, a proven constraint, a held-out SOTA with an honest benchmark-integrity statement. Pack B: a reproducible held-out result plus a ranked, calibrated, falsifiable prediction set. Full resolutions (Pack A) are windfalls; "solutions" (Pack B) are not on offer without a lab.
2. **Re-verify before every session.** This field reframes "solved" faster than any other: static single-domain folding was declared solved and the frontier moved to ensembles, affinity, and design within a year. Assume the same volatility everywhere.
3. **Distrust the verifier.** The signature Pack A failure is a biased simulator/benchmark producing confident-but-wrong "solutions" (binding free energy, docking, polymorph ranking are the usual suspects). Every Pack A prompt carries a benchmark-integrity clause with a held-out/prospective guard; honor it.
4. **Never launder a prediction as a proof.** Pack B reports state up front that the problem is reality-gated and label every in-silico design/prediction as a wet-lab-pending hypothesis. A leaderboard number is not a real-world guarantee.
5. **Leakage discipline is non-negotiable (Pack B).** Frozen train/test split committed before evaluation; homology/time separation documented; calibrated uncertainty on every prospective call.
6. **Leave a trail.** A paused line gets a `NEXT_STEPS.md` with the exact remaining obligations (the Moore-57 pattern from the mathematics program).
7. **Promote across the boundary when it moves.** When a trusted simulator or a dense frozen corpus appears, a Pack B problem can migrate to Pack A; renumber and note it here. The reverse (a Pack A verifier exposed as biased) demotes a problem to "confident-but-wrong risk" until the verifier is fixed.

## Escalation pattern per problem

- **Pack A:** reproduce the known frontier with our own verified toolchain → extend the certified frontier by the smallest nontrivial increment → mine the result for structure (the [sym]/[func] step) → attempt the resolution standard.
- **Pack B:** reproduce the SOTA baseline on a leakage-safe frozen split → certified method contribution or new held-out SOTA → calibrated, ranked, falsifiable prediction/design set → hand to a wet-lab partner; only physical results promote it further.

## Where to start

For a compute-only team this month, the sharpest Pack A entry points, each with machine-checkable ground truth available today, are **A02** (retrosynthesis against a verified route-checker), **A03** (MOF isotherm design with a GCMC verifier), **A05** (fine-tune a MACE-class potential toward CCSD(T) on a target chemistry), and **A01/A04** (formal N-representability constraints; symbolic reactivity laws). The Pack B items worth building the in-silico half of ahead of a collaborator are **B01** (variant effect, public clinical corpora) and **B02** (ΔΔG from public DMS data).
