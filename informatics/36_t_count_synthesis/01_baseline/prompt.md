### Abstract

The \(T\) gate is the dominant cost in every leading fault-tolerant architecture: Clifford gates are cheap, and a computation's resource bill is set almost entirely by how many \(T\) (or equivalent non-Clifford) gates it consumes. The problem here is exact and finite: given a unitary \(U\) that is exactly implementable over the Clifford+\(T\) gate set, determine the minimum number of \(T\) gates in any such circuit, and produce a circuit that meets it together with a machine-checkable proof of optimality. The Toffoli and Fredkin gates are settled - their \(T\)-count is exactly \(7\) - but the optimal \(T\)-count of most specific multi-qubit operators is open. This is a genuine AI-for-mathematics target: the exact-synthesis theory of Kliuchnikov–Maslov–Mosca and Gosset–Kliuchnikov–Mosca–Russo makes membership and cost decidable over the ring \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, i]\), and the search for a matching lower bound reduces to matroid / linear-algebra structure and to SAT/ILP that a workstation can drive and independently verify. The verifier that closes the loop is exact matrix reconstruction over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) plus a checked infeasibility proof for "no circuit with fewer \(T\) gates." Anything short of the section-2 standard - a good circuit with no matching lower bound, a numerically-optimized decomposition, a heuristic count - is a partial result, never a solution.

## 1. Exact problem statement

Fix the primitive eighth root of unity

\[
\omega \;=\; e^{i\pi/4},
\qquad
\mathbb{Z}[\omega] \;=\; \{\,a + b\omega + c\omega^2 + d\omega^3 : a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Z}\,\},
\qquad
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \;=\; \omega - \omega^3 .
\]

The single-qubit generators are

\[
H = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\omega\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S = T^2 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Z = T^4,
\]

together with \(\mathrm{CNOT}\) on ordered pairs of qubits.

The **Clifford group** \(\mathcal{C}_n\) on \(n\) qubits is generated by \(\{H, S, \mathrm{CNOT}\}\); the **Clifford+\(T\) set** adjoins \(T\). A unitary \(U \in U(2^n)\) is **exactly synthesizable** over Clifford+\(T\) iff it lies in the group generated by these gates. By the Giles–Selinger theorem this holds (with ancillas) iff every entry of \(U\) lies in \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, i]\) and \(U\) is unitary; ancilla-free exact synthesizability additionally requires the theorem's determinant/level conditions.

For an exactly synthesizable \(U\), a **Clifford+\(T\) circuit** for \(U\) is a finite word over

\[
\{\,H,\; S,\; \mathrm{CNOT},\; T,\; T^\dagger\,\}
\]

(acting on \(n\) qubits, no ancillas unless explicitly allowed) whose ordered product equals \(U\) exactly. The **\(T\)-count** of the circuit is the number of \(T\) and \(T^\dagger\) letters in the word. Define the cost measure

\[
\mathcal{T}(U) \;=\; \min\{\,\#T\text{-letters in }C \;:\; C \text{ is a Clifford+}T\text{ circuit with } C = U\,\}.
\]

Two regimes are in scope and must be kept distinct in every claim:

- **Ancilla-free \(\mathcal{T}(U)\):** no extra qubits are permitted.

- **Ancilla-assisted \(\mathcal{T}_a(U)\):** borrowed or clean ancillas are permitted, provided they are returned to their initial state; then \(\mathcal{T}_a(U) \le \mathcal{T}(U)\), and the two can differ strictly.

Every claim must state which regime, the qubit count \(n\), whether a global phase is quotiented out, and the exact target \(U\) as a matrix over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) (or as a named gate: Toffoli, Fredkin, \(\mathrm{CCZ}\), \(\mathrm{CS}\), a diagonal phase polynomial, or a specific reversible function's permutation matrix).

The open question, per target: **produce a circuit achieving some value \(t\) and a proof that no Clifford+\(T\) circuit for \(U\) uses fewer than \(t\) \(T\) gates.** Start from this prompt alone - the ring, the gate set, the regimes, and the cost measure are all fixed above.

## 2. Resolution standard

For a **named** target unitary \(U\) (declared explicitly as above, in a stated regime), resolution is a pair:

1. an explicit Clifford+\(T\) circuit \(C\) with \(t\) \(T\)-gates whose product, recomputed exactly over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\), equals \(U\) up to the declared global phase; and

2. a machine-checkable proof that \(\mathcal{T}(U) \ge t\) - that no Clifford+\(T\) circuit for \(U\) has \(T\)-count \(< t\).

**Named certified form.** The lower-bound proof must take one of the following forms.

- **Exact-synthesis optimum (GKMR).** Run the Gosset–Kliuchnikov–Mosca–Russo channel-representation search to completion for the \(T\)-budget \(t-1\), certifying by exhaustive exact enumeration over the relevant Clifford double-cosets that no exactly-synthesizable factorization of \(U\) with \(\le t-1\) \(T\)-gates exists; the enumeration is replayed by an independent checker over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\).

- **SAT/ILP optimality with a proof.** Encode "there is a Clifford+\(T\) circuit for \(U\) with \(\le t-1\) \(T\)-gates" (via the phase-polynomial / CNOT+\(T\) normal form or a bounded circuit template) as CNF or an integer program; obtain UNSAT with a DRAT/LRAT proof checked by an independent checker (or ILP infeasibility with an exact rational Farkas certificate); separately verify the \(t\)-gate witness reconstructs \(U\).

- **Matroid / Reed–Muller lower bound.** For a diagonal phase-polynomial target, a certified lower bound on the number of distinct odd-degree parities - equivalently a Reed–Muller minimum-distance / matroid-rank argument (Amy–Mosca) - with all arithmetic done exactly.

**Not accepted as resolution.**

- A circuit with a small \(T\)-count but **no matching lower bound** (the majority of "optimized" results).

- A **numerically** verified decomposition (floating-point equality to the target) without exact reconstruction over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\).

- A heuristic/greedy \(T\)-count from `T-par`, `TOpt`, PyZX, or a learned model reported as the optimum.

- An **unreplayable** solver run: a bare "UNSAT" with no proof trace, or a proof the independent checker cannot verify.

- An asymptotic \(O(\cdot)\) statement where an exact integer is asked.

- Conflating ancilla-free and ancilla-assisted counts, or dropping the global-phase convention.

## 3. Graded partial-result targets

- **P1 - Reproduce the settled frontier.** Re-derive \(\mathcal{T}(\text{Toffoli}) = \mathcal{T}(\text{Fredkin}) = 7\) with our own toolchain: a \(7\)-\(T\) circuit reconstructed exactly, plus an independent lower-bound proof (GKMR exhaustion or SAT UNSAT). Certificate: exact-matrix check + replayed infeasibility.

- **P2 - Small diagonal / single-qubit exact optima.** Certify \(\mathcal{T}(U)\) for a batch of exactly-synthesizable single-qubit \(U \in \langle H,T\rangle\) via the exact-synthesis normal form, matching the known minimal-\(T\) canonical word length. Certificate: canonical-form uniqueness proof + exact reconstruction.

- **P3 - Controlled-phase family.** Certify \(\mathcal{T}\) (both regimes) for \(\mathrm{CS}\), \(\mathrm{CCZ}\), and controlled-controlled-phase gates, stating exactly where ancillas help. Certificate: witness + SAT/ILP infeasibility at \(t-1\).

- **P4 - A specific 3-qubit reversible operator.** Pick a permutation unitary outside the literature's settled list; certify its ancilla-free \(\mathcal{T}\). Certificate: DRAT/LRAT-checked UNSAT for \(t-1\) plus exact witness.

- **P5 - A new exact value or improved lower bound.** Establish a previously-unknown \(\mathcal{T}(U)\) for a named 3–4 qubit \(U\), or raise a published lower bound for such a \(U\) with a checked infeasibility proof. Certificate: full manifest, both directions independently verified.

- **P6 - Phase-polynomial lower-bound tool.** A reusable, independently-audited matroid / Reed–Muller lower-bound routine emitting an exact certificate for diagonal targets, validated against P1–P3. Certificate: source + cross-check against SAT on shared instances.

## 4. Known results and prior art

- **Exact synthesis, single qubit.** Kliuchnikov, Maslov, Mosca, *Fast and efficient exact synthesis of single-qubit unitaries generated by Clifford and T gates*, QIC (2013, verify) - exact synthesizability iff entries lie in \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},i]\); minimal-\(T\) canonical words. Giles–Selinger extend to multi-qubit exact synthesis with ancillas (~2013, verify).

- **\(T\)-count algorithm.** Gosset, Kliuchnikov, Mosca, Russo, *An algorithm for the T-count*, QIC **14**, 1261–1276 (2014) - channel representation; proves Toffoli and Fredkin have \(T\)-count exactly \(7\), so the known 7-\(T\) circuits are optimal.

- **Matroid / Reed–Muller structure.** Amy, Maslov, Mosca, *Polynomial-time \(T\)-depth optimization of Clifford+\(T\) circuits via matroid partitioning*, IEEE TCAD **33**, 1476–1489 (2014). Amy, Mosca, *\(T\)-count optimization and Reed–Muller codes*, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory (~2019, verify) - links minimal \(T\)-count of phase polynomials to minimum-distance decoding of punctured Reed–Muller codes.

- **Heuristic optimizers (upper bounds only).** `T-par` (Amy–Maslov–Mosca); `TOpt` (Heyfron–Campbell, ~2018, verify); the `Feynman` toolkit (M. Amy); PyZX (Kissinger–van de Wetering) ZX-based \(T\)-count reduction. These give upper bounds, never optimality proofs.

- **Approximation (adjacent, not exact synthesis).** Ross, Selinger, *Optimal ancilla-free Clifford+\(T\) approximation of \(z\)-rotations*, QIC (~2016, verify) - `gridsynth`, optimal for a given \(\varepsilon\); Selinger's number-theoretic exact-synthesis machinery underpins it.

- **Recent search.** Reinforcement-learning Clifford+\(T\) synthesis (arXiv 2404.14865, 2024, verify) - upper bounds, not certified optima.

## 5. Attack plan

`[search]` `[cert]`. One workstation.

1. **Exact arithmetic core.** Implement \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) exactly (SageMath cyclotomic field \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)\), or FLINT). Every circuit-to-matrix product and equality test is exact; floating point only screens candidates.

2. **Upper bounds first.** Generate witness circuits with PyZX, `Feynman`, and hand constructions; record the best \(t\) per target, reconstruct each exactly, and store it as the certified upper bound.

3. **GKMR exact-synthesis search (small \(n\)).** Implement the channel representation and enumerate Clifford double-cosets to exhaust the \(t-1\) budget for single-, two-, and small-diagonal targets - the cleanest lower-bound engine for tiny operators.

4. **SAT/ILP lower bounds (3–4 qubits).** Encode a bounded template "\(\le t-1\) \(T\)-gates" (phase-polynomial normal form: CNOT network + parity-indexed \(T\) rotations) into CNF; drive kissat/CaDiCaL/CryptoMiniSat with DRAT/LRAT logging; check UNSAT with `drat-trim`/`lrat-check`. Mirror with an exact-rational ILP in SCIP/SoPlex and a Farkas certificate.

5. **Matroid / Reed–Muller route.** For diagonal targets compute the minimum number of odd parities via RM decoding; cross-check its value against the SAT result on shared instances.

6. **Failure modes.** Combinatorial blow-up of Clifford cosets past \(n=3\)–\(4\); CNF size exploding with the CNOT-network depth bound; unverified solver output (never trust a bare UNSAT); global-phase and ancilla-convention slips that make a "witness" the wrong operator; canonicity bugs in the double-coset enumeration. Keep every claim in the regime and phase convention declared in section 1.

## 6. Verification and auditability requirements

1. **Exact or certified computation.** All circuit products, equalities, and reconstructions are exact over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\); every lower bound carries a DRAT/LRAT UNSAT proof, an exact Farkas/ILP-infeasibility certificate, or a completed exact-synthesis exhaustion. Floating point is exploration only.

2. **Independent verification.** A standalone checker, written separately from the search, that (a) reconstructs the witness matrix and compares it to \(U\), and (b) replays the infeasibility proof (a DRAT/LRAT checker, a second solver, or a re-run of the coset exhaustion). Dual encodings (SAT and ILP) where the target admits both.

3. **Reproducibility.** Every encoding, solver name+version, seed, ancilla convention, and global-phase convention recorded; SHA-256 manifest over all circuits, matrices, CNF/LP files, and proof traces; the exact baseline value being matched or improved cited with source and access date.

4. **Preservation.** All search and reconstruction source code is part of the record; anything not preserved is stated explicitly (the Hadamard-668 lost-source lesson from the sister programs).

5. **Honest reporting.** The report states up front, per target and regime, whether both a witness and a matching lower bound were certified (full resolution), whether only an upper bound was found, and whether any published value was strictly improved - with the model and conventions fixed. A heuristic optimizer's output is never presented as \(\mathcal{T}(U)\).
