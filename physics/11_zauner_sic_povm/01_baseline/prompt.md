### Abstract

Zauner's conjecture asserts that in every dimension $d$ there exist $d^2$ equiangular complex lines in $\mathbb{C}^d$ - equivalently, a symmetric informationally complete POVM (SIC-POVM) - and, in its strong form, that one exists covariant under the Weyl–Heisenberg group with an order-3 Clifford (Zauner) symmetry. Exact solutions are known in a large but finite set of dimensions and reveal astonishing structure: the overlap phases generate specific abelian extensions (ray class fields) of the real quadratic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{(d-3)(d+1)})$ and appear to be expressible through Stark units, tying SIC existence to Hilbert's 12th problem. This is the program's premier *pattern-completion* problem: high-precision numerics, algebraic-number recognition, Galois-theoretic prediction, and exact verification in Pari/GP and SageMath, with each new exact dimension and each verified number-theoretic prediction a freestanding certified artifact. Recent dimension-tower and Stark-unit results have reportedly reached infinite families of dimensions - this frontier moves fast and must be re-verified before anything else. The complete resolution defined in section 2 is the target; anything less must be reported as a partial result, never represented as a solution.

## 1. Exact problem statement

A **SIC-POVM** in dimension $d$ is a set of $d^2$ unit vectors $\{|\psi_j\rangle\}_{j=1}^{d^2} \subset \mathbb{C}^d$ with

\[
|\langle \psi_j | \psi_k \rangle|^2 \;=\; \frac{1}{d+1} \qquad (j \ne k);
\]

equivalently, $d^2$ equiangular lines in $\mathbb{C}^d$. The operators $\tfrac{1}{d}|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|$ then form a POVM that is informationally complete. Two standard facts to re-derive in-session: $d^2$ is the maximum possible number of equiangular lines in $\mathbb{C}^d$, and at cardinality $d^2$ the common angle is forced to $|\langle\psi_j|\psi_k\rangle|^2 = 1/(d+1)$.

**Weyl–Heisenberg covariance.** Fix $\omega = e^{2\pi i/d}$, the shift $X|k\rangle = |k + 1 \bmod d\rangle$, the clock $Z|k\rangle = \omega^k |k\rangle$, and displacement operators

\[
D_{(a,b)} \;=\; \tau^{ab}\, X^a Z^b, \qquad \tau = -e^{\pi i/d},
\]

(the standard convention; fix it once and state it in every artifact - convention drift between $\tau$ and $\omega$ phases is a recurring source of error). A SIC is **WH-covariant** if it is the orbit $\{D_p |\phi\rangle\}_p$ of a **fiducial vector** $|\phi\rangle$. **Zauner symmetry:** an order-3 element of the Clifford group (the normalizer of the WH group in $U(d)$) conjecturally fixes a fiducial in every dimension; all known fiducials can be chosen in an eigenspace of a Zauner-conjugate.

**The problem (full Zauner conjecture): prove that a SIC-POVM exists for every integer $d \ge 2$.**

- Working form guiding all searches (the strong conjecture): for every $d$ there is a WH-covariant SIC with a Zauner-symmetric fiducial.
- What section 2 requires (the weak form): bare existence for every $d$.
- All known SICs are WH-covariant except the exceptional Hoggar lines in $d = 8$ (covariant under the three-qubit Pauli group).

**Number-theoretic superstructure (adopted as target predictions, not assumptions).** For $d \ge 4$ let $D$ be the squarefree part of $(d-3)(d+1)$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$. Empirically (Appleby–Flammia–McConnell–Yard and successors):

- the field generated by the SIC overlaps $\langle\phi|D_p|\phi\rangle$ is a specific abelian extension of $K$ - a ray class field with modulus tied to $d$ (conductor pattern known empirically);
- the Galois group acts on the set of WH-SIC orbits in a structured, predictable way;
- fiducials are constructible from Stark units (Kopp; Appleby–Bengtsson–Grassl–Harrison–McConnell).

These are precise, checkable predictions in each dimension, and verifying them in fresh dimensions is part of this prompt's target list.

## 2. Complete-resolution standard

A proof that SIC-POVMs exist in every dimension $d \ge 2$. Acceptable shapes:

- a uniform construction with a complete proof;
- a number-theoretic construction (e.g., Stark-unit-based) proved unconditionally for all $d$;
- an inductive/tower argument, conditional on nothing, covering all $d$.

Any computer-assisted steps must carry exact certificates - algebraic verification of the SIC equations in explicitly presented number fields - with independent checkers per section 6. A proof conditional on unproven conjectures (Stark's conjectures, GRH) is a major partial result at the P6 level, not a resolution, and must carry its hypotheses in every statement of it.

**Not accepted as resolution:**

- High-precision numerical fiducials - any number of digits, any number of dimensions - presented as existence proofs.
- Existence for infinitely many $d$ (a tower, or a family such as $d = n^2 + 3$) presented as "the conjecture"; it is flagship progress, but the conjecture is *every* $d$.
- Results conditional on Stark's conjectures or GRH presented as unconditional.
- Verification of Galois/ray-class-field predictions presented as existence progress per se (they are separate certified targets).
- Real equiangular-line results, approximate SICs, or general weighted 2-designs presented as SIC existence.
- "Existence for all $d \le X$ by exact solutions" presented as more than the finite statement it is.
