### Abstract

The kinematic fast-dynamo problem asks whether a smooth, steady, divergence-free velocity field can amplify a magnetic field at a growth rate bounded away from zero as the magnetic diffusivity $\eta\to0$. Numerics on ABC flows since Arnold–Korkina (1983) and Galloway–Frisch (1986) say yes; rigorous fast dynamos exist only for discontinuous or pulsed idealizations (Bayly, Childress–Gilbert theory), and Vishik's bound - ideal-limit dynamo growth is at most the flow's topological entropy - proves that non-chaotic steady flows cannot be fast. No smooth steady Euclidean flow has ever been *proven* fast, and, remarkably, dynamo action for the ABC flow appears never to have been certified rigorously at even one finite diffusivity. That last gap is precisely certifiable with current interval spectral methods, which is why this problem carries the `[cert]` mode: eigenvalue enclosures for an explicit non-self-adjoint operator, and computer-assisted chaos (entropy) bounds, are machine-checkable ground truth. Tier 4 calibration: the complete resolution in section 2 is unlikely; the graded targets in section 3, several of which would be firsts, are the goal, and no numerical growth rate may ever be represented as a proof.

## 1. Exact problem statement

### 1.1 Induction operator and growth rates

Let $u\in C^\infty(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb R^3)$, $\mathbb T^3=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3$, $\nabla\cdot u=0$, steady. For diffusivity $\eta>0$, the kinematic induction operator on divergence-free vector fields is
\[
L_\eta B=\nabla\times(u\times B)+\eta\,\Delta B ,
\qquad \nabla\cdot B=0 .
\]
Work on
\[
H:=\{B\in L^2(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb R^3):\ \nabla\cdot B=0\},
\]
optionally extended to Bloch fields $B=e^{iq\cdot x}b$ with $b$ periodic and $q\in[0,1)^3$; the sector must be declared, and the primary setting is $q=0$ (fields with the period of $u$). For $\eta>0$, $L_\eta$ has compact resolvent on $H$; define the growth rate
\[
\gamma(\eta):=\sup\{\operatorname{Re}\lambda:\ \lambda\in\sigma(L_\eta)\}.
\]
Verify in-session the equality of the spectral abscissa and the semigroup growth bound in this setting; state the argument rather than assuming it.

**Fast dynamo.** The flow $u$ is a *fast dynamo* if
\[
\liminf_{\eta\to0^+}\gamma(\eta)>0 ,
\]
and a *slow dynamo* if $\gamma(\eta)>0$ for small $\eta$ while the liminf is $0$.

### 1.2 The open problem (Arnold)

**Does there exist a smooth (real-analytic in the strong form), steady, divergence-free velocity field on $\mathbb T^3$ - or on a bounded domain of $\mathbb R^3$ with standard boundary conditions - that is a fast dynamo?**

Benchmark flow: the ABC family
\[
u_{ABC}=(A\sin z+C\cos y,\ B\sin x+A\cos z,\ C\sin y+B\cos x),
\qquad A=B=C=1 .
\]
Magnetic Reynolds number convention: $\mathrm{Rm}:=1/\eta$ under the normalization above; declare it once and keep it fixed.

### 1.3 The entropy ceiling

For smooth $u$, ideal-limit growth is constrained by topology:
\[
\limsup_{\eta\to0}\gamma(\eta)\ \le\ h_{\mathrm{top}}(u),
\]
where $h_{\mathrm{top}}(u)$ is the topological entropy of the time-one map of the flow of $u$ on $\mathbb T^3$ (adopt the Bowen $(n,\epsilon)$-spanning-set definition; state it in-session). This is the Vishik bound, with the rigorous smooth-flow form due to Klapper–Young (1995) - re-derive the hypotheses in-session. Hence integrable, zero-entropy flows are never fast, and any fast-dynamo candidate must be certifiably chaotic.

### 1.4 Symmetries of the benchmark

For $A=B=C$ the ABC flow is invariant under a $24$-element group of isometries of $\mathbb T^3$ (generated by coordinate cycling and combined shifts/reflections that preserve $u_{ABC}$). This group commutes with $L_\eta$ and decomposes $H$ into invariant blocks - a certified block decomposition both shrinks the certification problem and organizes the eigenvalue windows. Any use of it must include a proof of the decomposition, not an appeal to the physics literature.

### 1.5 Numerical landscape to be made rigorous (all values uncertified; verify each)

- First ABC(1,1,1) dynamo window: onset near $\mathrm{Rm}\approx8.9$, closure near $\mathrm{Rm}\approx17.5$ (Arnold–Korkina; Galloway–Frisch).
- Second window: from $\mathrm{Rm}\approx27$ onward, growth apparently persisting to $\mathrm{Rm}\sim2.5\times10^4$ with rate near $0.09$ (Bouya–Dormy).
- ABC Lagrangian chaos: principal vortices plus a chaotic web (Dombre et al.); no certified entropy value exists.

These decimals are targets for enclosure, never inputs to proofs.

## 2. Complete-resolution standard

A complete resolution is **either**:

1. An explicit smooth steady divergence-free $u$ on $\mathbb T^3$ (or a declared bounded domain), together with a proof - classical or computer-assisted with certificates per section 6 - that
   \[
   \liminf_{\eta\to0^+}\gamma(\eta)\ \ge\ c_0>0
   \]
   with an explicit constant $c_0$; **or**
2. A proof that no such flow exists: every smooth steady flow has $\liminf_{\eta\to0^+}\gamma(\eta)\le0$.

**Not accepted as resolution:**

- Numerical growth rates at finite $\mathrm{Rm}$, however large (including Bouya–Dormy-type computations at $\mathrm{Rm}\sim2.5\times10^4$), or extrapolations thereof.
- Fast dynamos for time-periodic, pulsed, stochastic, or discontinuous velocity models (Bayly–Childress pulsed waves, stretch–fold–shear maps, baker's-map models) presented as the steady smooth case.
- Growth in the ideal limit $\eta=0$ - frozen-field stretching, flux growth, Lyapunov-exponent arguments - presented as a fast dynamo: positivity of the $\eta=0$ operator's growth does not imply $\liminf_{\eta\to0}\gamma(\eta)>0$.
- Anosov-flow constructions on curved 3-manifolds (Arnold's suspension example) presented as Euclidean or toroidal resolutions.
- Growth along a sequence $\eta_j\to0$ without control between the $\eta_j$, presented as the liminf statement; it is a valuable partial and must be labeled as such.
- Undeclared switches of norm or Bloch sector between claims.

## 3. Graded partial-result targets

- **P1 - Certified dynamo action for the ABC flow at one finite Rm (likely a first).**
  - Deliverable: an interval-verified eigenvalue enclosure proving $\gamma(\eta)>0$ for $u_{ABC}$, $A{=}B{=}C{=}1$, at one explicit $\eta$ inside the first numerically known dynamo window ($\mathrm{Rm}$ roughly $9$–$17.5$; verify the window values).
  - Certificate: finite Galerkin block plus an interval Schur-complement bound on the infinite tail (using $\eta\Delta$ coercivity and explicit convective-term bounds), plus a verified Newton/Krawczyk enclosure of the unstable eigenpair - checkable from the certificate file alone.
  - Verify the novelty claim against the literature before publishing any "first rigorous steady smooth kinematic dynamo" statement.
- **P2 - Certified window boundaries.**
  - Deliverable: parametric-in-$\eta$ enclosures - certified $\gamma(\eta)>0$ on an explicit interval of $\mathrm{Rm}$, and certified $\gamma(\eta)<0$ below the window - establishing the window structure (Arnold–Korkina onset; second window onset near $\mathrm{Rm}\approx27$, verify) as a theorem rather than a plot.
- **P3 - Certified chaos and the entropy ceiling for ABC.**
  - (a) A CAPD covering-relation/cone-condition proof of a horseshoe for a Poincaré return map of the ABC flow, yielding a certified $h_{\mathrm{top}}\ge h_0>0$ with explicit $h_0$.
  - (b) A certified upper bound on $h_{\mathrm{top}}$ from interval bounds on stretching-rate integrals (crude is acceptable).
  - Combined with Vishik–Klapper–Young: a certified ceiling on any possible fast-dynamo rate for ABC. Check first whether a computer-assisted chaos proof for ABC(1,1,1) already exists (verify); if so, reproduce and extend rather than duplicate.
- **P4 - High-Rm certified frontier.**
  - Deliverable: P1-style enclosures pushed to $\mathrm{Rm}\in[10^2,10^3{+}]$ on a logarithmic grid, with reported enclosure widths - a *rigorous* version of the lower range of the Galloway–Frisch/Bouya–Dormy growth curve, plus a quantified account of how non-normality inflates certification cost with $\mathrm{Rm}$.
  - Any certified uniform positive lower bound on $\gamma$ over a full decade of $\mathrm{Rm}$ is a strong partial.
- **P5 - Rigorous fast dynamo in a limiting regime for a steady smooth flow.**
  - Deliverable: uniform-in-$\eta$ growth along an explicit sequence $\eta_j\to0$, or in an asymptotic regime (large-amplitude shear or boundary-layer limits in the Soward–Childress tradition; a smoothed stretch–fold–shear mechanism embedded in a steady flow), with computer-assisted error control.
  - This is the strongest realistic analytic target - "fast along a certified sequence" - and must be labeled as short of the liminf standard.
- **P6 - Obstruction sharpenings.**
  - Deliverable: quantitative improvements of the entropy bound (e.g., diffusive corrections $\gamma(\eta)\le h_{\mathrm{top}}-c(\eta)$ in declared classes), or new certified anti-dynamo theorems for explicit steady-flow families - extensions of Cowling/Zeldovich to certified near-planar or near-integrable classes with interval-verified constants.

## 4. Known results and prior art

- Arnold–Korkina (1983): first ABC(1,1,1) eigenvalue computations and the dynamo window. Arnold (1981–83); Arnold–Zeldovich–Ruzmaikin–Sokoloff (1981): rigorous fast dynamo for a uniformly stretching flow on a compact 3-manifold with non-Euclidean metric - the geometric prototype, not a Euclidean answer.
- Childress (1970): ABC dynamo theory. Dombre–Frisch–Greene–Hénon–Mehr–Soward (1986): ABC Lagrangian chaos (numerical). Galloway–Frisch (1986): sustained ABC growth at moderate Rm. Bouya–Dormy (~2013): ABC growth to $\mathrm{Rm}\sim2.5\times10^4$ with rate near $0.09$ (verify).
- Idealized fast dynamos: Bayly (1986); Bayly–Childress (1988–89), pulsed dynamos; Gilbert (1988–93), Ponomarenko-type and stretch–fold–shear analyses (the smoothed Ponomarenko dynamo is slow - verify); Soward (1987): Roberts-flow growth $\sim\log\log\mathrm{Rm}/\log\mathrm{Rm}$, almost fast (verify the exact form); Childress–Gilbert, *Stretch, Twist, Fold: The Fast Dynamo* (1995) - the canonical monograph; Oseledets (~1993) on discontinuous fast models (verify).
- G. O. Roberts (1972): dynamo action for spatially periodic cellular flows - the classical positive slow-dynamo existence result closest in spirit to P1.
- Upper bounds: Vishik (1989) - ideal-limit growth bounded by topological entropy; Klapper–Young (1995, Comm. Math. Phys.) - the rigorous entropy bound for smooth flows; the reason zero-entropy steady flows are never fast.
- Anti-dynamo theorems: Cowling (1933), axisymmetric; Zeldovich (1957), planar.
- Surveys: Gilbert, "Dynamo theory", Handbook of Mathematical Fluid Dynamics (~2003) (verify); Fearn–Roberts-style reviews of kinematic dynamo spectra (verify).
- Adjacent chaotic-advection rigor: Finn–Ott (1988) on chaotic fast-dynamo maps; nonintegrability results for ABC-type flows (Ziglin tradition, ~1990s) (verify what exactly is proved for $A{=}B{=}C{=}1$).
- Verified-numerics methodology (imported): interval eigenvalue enclosures (Rump; the Nakao–Plum–Watanabe school); CAPD covering relations for chaos proofs (Zgliczyński and collaborators). No certified ABC dynamo action and no certified ABC horseshoe located as of drafting (verify both claims).

## 5. Attack plan

1. **`[cert]` Galerkin-plus-tail enclosure (P1, P2, P4).** Fourier basis on $\mathbb T^3$; $u_{ABC}$ couples mode $k$ only to $k\pm e_i$, so the matrix is sparse and structured. Split $H=H_{\le N}\oplus H_{>N}$:
   - on the tail, $\eta|k|^2$ dominates the convective bound $\|B\mapsto\nabla\times(u\times B)\|\le C_u(1+|k|)$, with $C_u$ computed exactly for ABC - certified tail resolvent bound;
   - reduce to the finite block by interval Schur complement;
   - enclose the leading eigenpair by a verified Newton/Krawczyk step around a float approximation (ARPACK/SLEPc exploration, then a C++ interval pass with Arb/Eigen).
   Workstation sizing: $N\approx20$–$40$ (of order $10^5$–$10^6$ complex dimensions) fits in 64 GB with sparse interval algebra at moderate Rm; expect the required $N$ to grow like $\mathrm{Rm}^{1/2}$-to-$\mathrm{Rm}^{3/4}$ (document empirically).
2. **`[cert]` Chaos certification (P3).** Poincaré section for the ABC flow (e.g., the $z=0$ plane, boxes near numerically known heteroclinic tangles); CAPD $C^0$/$C^1$ rigorous integration; covering relations plus cone conditions $\Rightarrow$ symbolic dynamics $\Rightarrow$ $h_{\mathrm{top}}\ge(\log2)/T_{\max}$ with a certified return-time bound. Seed box locations from float orbit computations of the chaotic web; the certification run must not depend on the seeds being accurate.
3. **`[proof]` Limiting-regime analysis (P5, P6).** Symbolic boundary-layer/WKB constructions (SymPy) for shear-dominated steady families with interval-bounded error terms; entropy-bound sharpening along the Klapper–Young proof skeleton with explicit constants.
4. **Order of work.** P1 before everything - it validates the entire certification stack; P3 in parallel on an independent toolchain; P4 only after P1's cost curve is measured; P5/P6 are analysis tracks that should start from the P3 entropy numbers.
5. **Expected failure modes.**
   - Non-normality: the distance from a float eigenvalue to a certifiable enclosure can explode through pseudospectral sensitivity; when the Krawczyk step fails, enlarge the Galerkin block, not the interval.
   - Eigenvalue collisions and complex pairs near window edges: track the full leading cluster, not a single eigenvalue.
   - Symmetry reduction done wrong: ABC symmetries partition the spectrum - exploit them only with a proved block decomposition (section 1.4).
   - Bloch-sector blindness: the fastest-growing mode may live at $q\neq0$; a certificate at $q=0$ proves dynamo action but says nothing about the supremum over sectors - label every claim with its sector.
   - Long Poincaré return times inflating CAPD error: choose sections crossing the strong-stretching region, keep boxes small, budget days of compute.
   - Horseshoe entropy far below true $h_{\mathrm{top}}$: the certified ceiling of P3(b) and the certified floor of P3(a) will not meet; report the bracket, never a point value.
