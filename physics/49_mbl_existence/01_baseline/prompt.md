### Abstract

Many-body localization - the persistence of non-thermalizing, quasi-local integrals of motion in disordered interacting spin chains - sits in an unusual epistemic position: Imbrie's 2016 proof of MBL for a random-field Ising-type chain is rigorous *modulo* a physically plausible but unproven level-statistics assumption, while the numerical community now genuinely contests whether the phase survives the thermodynamic limit at all (Šuntajs–Bonča–Prosen–Vidmar drift analyses versus Sierant–Zakrzewski and others; De Roeck–Huveneers avalanche instability as the proposed destruction mechanism - verify the current state of this fast-moving debate). Settling the question **either way** counts as resolution. This Tier 4 prompt does not expect a settlement; its realistic products are: a precise formalization of Imbrie's assumption and the lattice of its minimal strengthenings and weakenings; certified small-chain spectral statistics as machine-checked ground truth in a debate currently run on uncertified numerics; rigorous analysis of the toy avalanche models, which appear fully tractable and whose resolution would turn the destabilization heuristic into mathematics; and conditional theorems mapping assumption-space. The complete resolution defined in section 2 is the target, and anything less must be reported as a partial result, never represented as a solution.

## 1. Exact problem statement

Adopt Imbrie's model class as the reference (state any deviation explicitly). On $\mathcal{H}_n = \bigotimes_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{C}^2$:

\[
H_n(\omega) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} h_i\, S_i^z \;+\; \gamma \sum_{i=1}^{n} S_i^x \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} J_i\, S_i^z S_{i+1}^z ,
\qquad S^{x,z} = \tfrac12 \sigma^{x,z},
\]

with $\{h_i\}$ i.i.d. with bounded density on $[-1, 1]$, $\{J_i\}$ i.i.d. likewise (or fixed $J$ - fix the ensemble per artifact), and transverse field $\gamma$ small. The companion model behind most numerics is the random-field Heisenberg chain

\[
H \;=\; \sum_i \mathbf{S}_i\cdot\mathbf{S}_{i+1} \;+\; \sum_i h_i S_i^z, \qquad h_i \sim \mathrm{Unif}[-W, W].
\]

Results for one model must never be silently transferred to the other.

Diagnostic statistics used throughout (defined here so every artifact shares them): for a sorted finite spectrum $E_1 \le \dots \le E_N$ with gaps $s_a = E_{a+1} - E_a$, the gap ratios are

\[
r_a \;=\; \frac{\min(s_a, s_{a+1})}{\max(s_a, s_{a+1})} \in [0, 1],
\]

with ensemble-averaged $\langle r \rangle \approx 0.3863$ for Poisson and $\approx 0.5307$ for GOE (verify digits); the minimal splitting is $\min_a s_a$. These are finite-size diagnostics, never phase definitions.

"Thermodynamic-limit phase" means: the defining property holds with disorder-probability one, with all constants (localization lengths, tail bounds) uniform in $n$ along $n \to \infty$ - not merely at each finite $n$, and not merely for $n$ below some disorder-dependent crossover scale. Every claim must state which of these it establishes.

Definitions of MBL - fix them; the debate is partly definitional:

- **(LIOM form)** For given $(\gamma, \text{ensemble})$: with probability one there exists a quasi-local unitary $U(\omega)$ - generated by local terms with exponentially decaying tails, with constants uniform in $n$ - such that $U^\dagger H_n U$ is diagonal in the $S^z$ product basis; equivalently a complete set of exponentially quasi-local integrals of motion exists.
- **(Dynamical form)** Almost surely: absence of transport and thermalization - time-averaged local observables retain initial-condition memory for typical product states uniformly in $n$; eigenstates violate ETH and obey area-law entanglement.
- **(Spectral form; Imbrie's conclusion)** Eigenstates labeled by LIOM configurations with exponentially decaying dressing; Poisson-like level statistics.

**Target determination.** Decide, with proof, whether there exists $\gamma_0 > 0$ (respectively $W_0 < \infty$) such that the LIOM form holds in the thermodynamic limit for $0 < \gamma < \gamma_0$ - **or** prove the negation (e.g. an avalanche-instability theorem: for all $\gamma > 0$ the LIOM form fails almost surely in the thermodynamic limit). Either resolution counts.

Imbrie's assumption, to be formalized exactly in-session (that formalization is target P1; stated informally here): **LLA($\nu$, $C$), limited level attraction** - throughout the multiscale diagonalization scheme, finite-volume eigenvalue splittings satisfy bounds of the shape

\[
\mathbb{P}\big( \min |E_a - E_b| < \delta \big) \;\le\; \delta^{\nu}\, C^{\,n}
\]

at each scale, with the precise quantification over subsystems, scales, and conditioning to be extracted faithfully from Imbrie (2016) - from the paper itself, not from folklore paraphrases.

## 2. Complete-resolution standard

A complete resolution is either:

- **(a)** an unconditional proof of the LIOM form for the stated model at some explicit $\gamma_0 > 0$ - Imbrie's theorem with the level-statistics assumption discharged or circumvented; or
- **(b)** a proof that MBL fails in the thermodynamic limit for the stated model class for all $\gamma > 0$, with the failure notion stated in both LIOM and dynamical forms - e.g. a rigorous avalanche argument covering the actual spin chain, not only toy models.

All probabilistic and spectral steps fully proved; every computational ingredient certified with independent checkers.

**Not accepted as resolution:**

- Numerical claims in either direction - level-statistics drifts, entanglement growth, Thouless-time analyses - at any chain length, certified or not (certified numerics are P-target ground truth, never a resolution).
- Imbrie's theorem restated *with* its assumption, presented as unconditional.
- Toy-model results (avalanche toys, random regular graphs, Fock-space caricatures, Floquet variants) presented as statements about the spin chain - they are P-targets.
- Results for other ensembles (quasi-periodic fields, correlated disorder, infinite-randomness constructions) passed off as the i.i.d. random-field problem.
- Prethermal statements ("MBL-like behavior up to exponentially long times" - true, proved, and not the question) represented as the thermodynamic-limit phase.
- Redefinitions of MBL weak enough to be vacuously true or strong enough to be trivially false, unless the definitional analysis itself is the explicitly labeled deliverable.
- Certified finite-$n$ level statistics - however clean - presented as deciding the thermodynamic-limit question in either direction; the entire controversy is about the $n \to \infty$ extrapolation.

## 4. Known results and prior art

- Anderson (1958); Basko–Aleiner–Altshuler (2006): the physics foundation of MBL; perturbative, not rigorous.
- Pal–Huse (2010): the numerical phase diagram for the random-field Heisenberg chain that fixed the community's reference model and its estimated critical $W_c \approx 3.5$–$4$ (a finite-size estimate now contested - verify).
- Nandkishore–Huse (2015); Serbyn–Papić–Abanin (2013), Huse–Nandkishore–Oganesyan (2014): LIOM phenomenology - the structures Imbrie's theorem makes rigorous, conditionally.
- Imbrie (2016, J. Stat. Phys., with companion PRL): MBL for the random-field Ising-type chain, conditional on limited level attraction - the central object of this prompt. Imbrie–Ros–Scardicchio (c. 2017): LIOM review.
- De Roeck–Huveneers (c. 2017): avalanche/ergodic-bubble instability scenario; Luitz–Huveneers–De Roeck (2017): numerical avalanche seeding; Thiery–Huveneers–Müller–De Roeck (c. 2018): quantitative avalanche theory (verify).
- De Roeck–Huveneers–Müller–Schiulaz (c. 2016): absence of MBL in dimensions $d \ge 2$ argued via the same bubble mechanism - the 1D case survives it only marginally, which is why the toy models of P3 are decisive.
- Šuntajs–Bonča–Prosen–Vidmar (c. 2020): spectral-form-factor/Thouless-time drift analysis questioning the phase; follow-ups sharpening it.
- Sierant–Zakrzewski and collaborators (c. 2020–2023): counter-analyses supporting a transition at larger $W$; Morningstar–Colmenarez–Khemani–Luitz–Huse (c. 2022): many-body-resonance reinterpretation; Sels; Polkovnikov (c. 2021+): dynamical obstructions. - Elgart–Klein–Stolz (c. 2018+): droplet-spectrum localization for random XXZ - unconditional theorems in a restricted energy window; Beaud–Warzel (c. 2017): related droplet results.
- Abanin–De Roeck–Ho–Huveneers (c. 2017): rigorous prethermalization (exponentially long times) - the proved part of slow dynamics.

- Experiments (Bloch group quantum-gas microscopes, c. 2015+; trapped ions): finite-time, finite-size observations - context only, decisive for nothing here.
- Oganesyan–Huse (2007): the gap-ratio diagnostic $\langle r \rangle$ - the statistic P2 certifies.
- Wegner (1981); Minami (1996): the one-body level-statistics toolbox whose many-body analogue is exactly what LLA needs; the failure of naive many-body Wegner/Minami bounds is the technical crux - document it precisely inside P1.
